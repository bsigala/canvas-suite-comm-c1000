Course Description and Student Learning Outcomes

In this course, students learn and apply foundational rhetorical theories and techniques of public speaking in a multicultural democratic society. Students discover, develop, and critically analyze ideas in public discourse through research, reasoning, organization, composition, delivery to a live audience and evaluation of various types of speeches, including informative and persuasive speeches. This course is designed for Communication Studies majors and recommended for students interested in the Forensics: Speech and Debate team, and/or Pathway to Law.

By taking this class, you should achieve the following key **Student Learning Outcomes (SLOs)**:

  • In an oral presentation, students will effectively implement appropriate content and research.
  • In an oral presentation, students will effectively organize speech content.
  • In an oral presentation, students will display effective speech delivery.

To reach these outcomes, our weekly course loop will involve conducting academic research, writing structured outlines, creating visual aids, and recording and submitting speech videos.

Student Expectations & Class Policies

Drop Policy

Consistent participation is vital in an online course. Your consistent submission of weekly assignments is how I verify your attendance and active enrollment. If you do not submit work for two consecutive weeks, it is considered a basis for being dropped from the course.

Deadlines & Late Work

By default, late work receives a score of 0. I understand that emergencies happen, so I accept late submissions only if you provide formal documentation of extenuating circumstances. Additionally, any approved late submissions must be submitted in a reasonable timeframe close to the original deadline.

Respect & Civility

We are here to support each other as we conquer speech anxiety and build technical communication skills. Negative, personal, or insulting remarks regarding another student's ability, character, or background are strictly unacceptable. Always provide positive, constructive, and encouraging critiques.

Class Topics & Discourse

Critical thinking involves engaging with diverse, challenging, or controversial viewpoints. You are expected to examine these issues thoughtfully. If any specific topic triggers extreme personal discomfort, please let me know so we can discuss alternative accommodations.

Artificial Intelligence (AI) Guidelines

Artificial Intelligence tools can be used to brainstorm, refine outlines, and practice speech structures. However, you must always cite your use of AI and ensure that all final outlines and delivered content are entirely your own and meet our academic standards.

Assignment Descriptions

You can expect to complete the following core types of work throughout the semester to assess your speaking, writing, and analytical skills:

  • Participation (Weekly): Short interactive check-ins, worksheets, and exercises graded primarily on active engagement, critical reflection, and timely completion.
  • Discussions: Structured async conversations where you post your initial ideas (often video recordings) and respond to classmates using our specialized peer-commenting models.
  • Quizzes: Online checks to verify your understanding of key terminology, outline mechanics, and chapter readings.
  • Preparation Outlines: Full-sentence, structured blueprints (500+ words) that map out your speech introductions, main points with subpoints, and bibliography pages. Graded on formatting, logical structure, and source citations.
  • Presentation Aids: Visual tools (slide decks, whiteboards, handouts) created to support your speeches. Graded on design, relevance, clarity, and integration with your speaking.
  • Speeches: Polished 4-to-6 minute video recordings where you inform, persuade, or introduce topics. Graded on nonverbal delivery, verbal variety, structure, and oral citations.
